# PIP-v1 Image Generation Style Guide

## Purpose

PIP-v1 is a clean, simple, flat 2D vector illustration style designed
for educational and e-learning artwork. Use this guide as the default
visual specification when generating new PIP-v1 illustrations on an
account or project that does not already know the style.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Core Visual Style

PIP-v1 should feel:

-   Clean and polished
-   Flat rather than painterly
-   Vector-like
-   Simple and readable
-   Friendly and youthful
-   Suitable for educational materials
-   Minimally detailed
-   Consistent across characters and scenes

Avoid realism, 3D rendering, painterly shading, excessive texture, and
highly detailed cartoon rendering.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. Shapes and Linework

Use simple, controlled shapes.

Shapes should generally have:

-   Clean silhouettes
-   Smooth but restrained curves
-   Relatively simple geometry
-   Limited small details
-   Clear separation between objects
-   A vector-rigged feeling for characters

Avoid excessive roundness. Characters should not look as though every
body part was constructed from circles or inflated shapes.

### Curves

Use curves where they improve the silhouette, but keep them restrained.

Suitable uses include:

-   Slightly rounded shoulders
-   Natural head curves
-   Simplified curved hair masses

Avoid:

-   Overly curved torsos
-   Tube-like sleeves
-   Rubbery limbs
-   Unnecessary clothing curves

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. Character Construction

Characters should feel somewhat like 2D rigging models.

The torso/body should remain comparatively stable and structured while
the arms and hands communicate the action.

Poses should read clearly without requiring exaggerated body
deformation.

### Shoulders

Use:

-   Relaxed shoulders
-   Slight natural rounding
-   Clean transitions into the arms

Avoid:

-   Extremely rounded shoulders
-   Stiff raised shoulders
-   Bulbous shoulder shapes

The desired balance is structured but relaxed.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. Clothing

Clothing construction is an important characteristic of PIP-v1.

Keep clothing:

-   Flat
-   Simple
-   Clean
-   Minimally folded
-   Geometrically controlled

Avoid unnecessary:

-   Fabric folds
-   Wrinkles
-   Seams
-   Highlights
-   Shadows
-   Internal clothing details

### Sleeves

For short sleeves, use a simple straight or nearly straight ending line.

**Do not draw the sleeve opening as a circular or elliptical tube.**

**Do not show a dark inner sleeve opening.**

The preferred construction is:

**shirt shape → simple sleeve edge → arm**

Avoid:

**shirt → rounded sleeve tube → dark interior → arm**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. Hands

Hands should be highly simplified.

Use:

-   Simple silhouettes
-   Minimal finger separation
-   Only enough detail to communicate the gesture
-   Clean vector-like shapes

Avoid:

-   Individually articulated fingers unless necessary
-   Fingernails
-   Knuckles
-   Realistic joints
-   Hand wrinkles
-   Complicated gripping anatomy

When a hand holds an object, prioritize readability of the action over
anatomical realism.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6. Arms and Body Movement

Keep the main body comparatively stable while allowing the arms to
perform the action.

Think:

**simple character rig + clear arm gesture**

rather than:

**fully animated body + exaggerated curves and deformation**

This is especially important when characters are:

-   Holding something
-   Reading
-   Pointing
-   Fishing
-   Carrying an object
-   Looking at something in their hands

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 7. Faces

Faces should be simple and highly readable.

Use minimal features:

-   Simple eyes
-   Simple eyebrows when needed
-   Small/simple nose treatment
-   Simple mouth
-   Minimal facial linework

Avoid:

-   Realistic facial anatomy
-   Detailed lips
-   Unnecessary eyelashes
-   Detailed noses
-   Skin rendering
-   Complex highlights and shadows

### Optional Established Color Treatment

For the established PIP-v1 character treatment, facial-feature linework
can use:

**HEX #993952**

Treat this as an optional palette specification rather than a required
structural feature of the style.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 8. Hair

Treat hair primarily as simple graphic masses.

Use:

-   Recognizable silhouettes
-   Limited internal lines
-   Simple sections
-   Restrained texture

Avoid:

-   Individual strands
-   Excessive curls
-   Many highlight lines
-   Realistic hair texture
-   Overly complicated hairstyles

Even textured or curly hair should be simplified into larger readable
shapes.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 9. Color

Use a controlled flat-color palette.

Prefer:

-   Solid fills
-   Clear color separation
-   Limited palettes
-   Harmonious colors
-   Backgrounds lighter than foreground characters

Avoid:

-   Unnecessary gradients
-   Glossy rendering
-   Dramatic lighting
-   Realistic shadows
-   Complex color variation inside one object

The foreground character or object should remain visually dominant.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 10. Shading

Keep shading minimal or absent.

Default toward flat color.

If shading is necessary for separation, it should be:

-   Simple
-   Subtle
-   Graphic
-   Limited to large shapes

Avoid:

-   Realistic cast shadows
-   Airbrushed shading
-   Cinematic lighting
-   Complex ambient lighting
-   Shiny highlights

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 11. Backgrounds

Backgrounds should support the scene without competing with the
character.

Use:

-   Simplified environmental shapes
-   Sparse detail
-   Light colors
-   Simple line art
-   Large clean areas

Some scenes may use an almost duotone background, such as white plus one
secondary palette color.

Backgrounds can rely more heavily on line art than filled detail.

Preferred visual hierarchy:

**foreground character/object = strongest**

**background = lighter and simpler**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 12. Educational Illustration Priority

Every visual should communicate its teaching point quickly.

Prioritize:

1.  Clear action
2.  Clear expression
3.  Recognizable object
4.  Readable silhouette
5.  Simple composition
6.  Decorative detail last

If a decorative element does not help communicate the scene, remove it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 13. Object Design

Objects should follow the same simplification rules as characters.

Use:

-   Recognizable silhouettes
-   Simple geometry
-   Flat fills
-   Minimal decoration
-   Few internal lines

Avoid automatically adding:

-   Sparkles
-   Floral designs
-   Decorative borders
-   Unnecessary icons
-   Ornamental patterns

Modern educational objects should look simple and contemporary unless
the requested scene specifically requires an older, fantasy, historical,
or decorative design.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 14. Composition

Prefer compositions that immediately communicate the required action.

Possible framing includes:

-   Full body
-   Waist-up
-   Bust-up
-   Shoulder-up
-   Close-up of hands and objects

When the educational focus is an object or action, crop closer rather
than showing unnecessary parts of the character.

If the important action is examining, holding, reading, or using an
object, a close view of the hands and object may be stronger than
showing the whole person.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 15. Camera Angles

PIP-v1 does not require one fixed camera angle.

Alternative angles are encouraged when they:

-   Clarify an action
-   Make a sequence less repetitive
-   Emphasize an important object
-   Create a useful close-up

Changing the angle should not change the established character design or
drawing style.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 16. Reference-Image Preservation

When a reference image is supplied and the instruction is to change only
one element, preserve everything else as closely as possible.

This may include:

-   Pose
-   Composition
-   Character proportions
-   Background
-   Clothing
-   Hairstyle
-   Camera angle
-   Expression
-   Object placement

Do not redesign or "improve" unrelated elements.

If asked to change only the character, keep the background unchanged.

If asked to change only an object, do not redesign the character.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 17. Transparent-Background Assets

For isolated educational assets:

-   Preserve the foreground exactly where requested
-   Use clean, smooth edges
-   Remove all background material
-   Keep objects fully intact
-   Use true transparency

Do not add:

-   White backgrounds
-   Drop shadows
-   Halos
-   Replacement scenery

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 18. What PIP-v1 Should NOT Look Like

Avoid making PIP-v1 look:

-   Realistic
-   Semi-realistic
-   3D
-   Painterly
-   Heavily textured
-   Anime-like
-   Overly cute/chibi
-   Excessively rounded
-   Rubber-hose-like
-   Heavily shaded
-   Anatomically detailed
-   Sketchy
-   Ornate

### Important recurring failure modes to avoid

-   Dark inner sleeve openings
-   Circular sleeve ends
-   Too many finger details
-   Overly rounded shoulders
-   Excessive body curvature
-   Complicated hair details
-   Unnecessary clothing details

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 19. Master Prompt for Other Accounts

Use the following prompt when an account or project does not already
know PIP-v1:

> Create a clean, flat 2D vector-style educational illustration. Use
> simple geometric shapes, smooth clean edges, solid color fills,
> minimal or no shading, restrained curves, and a limited harmonious
> palette. Characters should have simple readable faces, simplified
> hair, relaxed slightly rounded shoulders, structured torsos, and
> highly simplified hands with minimal finger detail. Clothing should be
> clean and flat with almost no folds. Short sleeves must end with a
> simple straight line; do not show a circular sleeve opening or dark
> inner sleeve area. Keep the character somewhat like a 2D rigging
> model: the torso stays relatively stable while the arms communicate
> the action. Use light, simplified backgrounds with sparse detail or
> simple line art so the foreground remains dominant. Avoid realism, 3D
> rendering, gradients, painterly texture, detailed anatomy, excessive
> curves, complicated hair, ornate decoration, and unnecessary visual
> details. The final image should feel polished, friendly, modern,
> simple, and appropriate for children's educational e-learning
> materials.

For the established facial-feature color treatment, optionally add:

> Use HEX #993952 for the character's facial-feature linework.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 20. Quick QA Checklist

Before accepting a PIP-v1 image, check:

### Character

-   Is the silhouette clean and simple?
-   Is the torso structured rather than overly curved?
-   Are the shoulders relaxed with only slight rounding?
-   Are the hands simplified?
-   Is the hair represented with large, simple masses?

### Clothing

-   Are folds and seams minimal?
-   Are short-sleeve endings simple and flat?
-   Is there no dark inner sleeve opening?
-   Does the clothing remain vector-like?

### Rendering

-   Are colors mostly solid and flat?
-   Is shading minimal or absent?
-   Are gradients, glossy effects, and realistic lighting avoided?
-   Is the linework clean?

### Background

-   Is it lighter and simpler than the foreground?
-   Does it contain only necessary detail?
-   Does it support rather than compete with the main action?

### Composition

-   Is the requested action immediately understandable?
-   Is the important object clearly visible?
-   Is the framing appropriate for the educational focus?

### Consistency

-   If a reference was supplied, were unchanged elements preserved?
-   Does the image remain consistent with established PIP-v1 proportions
    and simplification?
-   Were unnecessary decorative details avoided?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Core Principle

**Keep everything as simple, flat, clean, and readable as possible while
preserving enough character, expression, and action to communicate the
educational scene clearly.**
